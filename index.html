Plan summary

This week is built for Electric Forest 2027. Lift Monday through Friday. Rest Saturday and Sunday. The two sessions that stay every week are goblet squat and glute on Monday and full-body kettlebell on Friday. Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday fill the gaps with bracing, hinging, and one-arm work.

Every lifting day uses a kettlebell. Walking is the cardio. No running, jumping, or swings. The aim is a quieter nerve, stronger hips, and 135 pounds by April 2027 — so June in Rothbury feels easy.

Estimated energy

Female, 29 years, 5'1", 165 lb. Resting burn is about 1,411 calories. With five days of lifting plus easy walking, total daily burn is likely 1,940–2,190 calories.

Two thousand four hundred calories a day is probably at or above maintenance at this size and activity level. That intake will fuel training. On its own, it is unlikely to produce 30 pounds of fat loss. If 135 lb by April is the priority, keep protein at 150–165 g and bring the rest of the plate to about 1,750–1,850 calories. Weigh in for two weeks and let the trend decide. Details are on the Nutrition tab.

This phase

Six non-negotiables

1. Neutral spine under load

The low back does not round or arch to lift a weight. Hips and shoulders move. The lumbar spine stays quiet.

2. Hips do the bending

Pick things up with a hip hinge, not a toe-touch. If the dowel leaves your head, mid-back, or tailbone, the hinge is lost.

3. Core resists motion

Bird dog, dead bug, McGill curl-up, Pallof press, and side plank. No sit-ups. No Russian twists.

4. Walking is the cardio

Easy treadmill or outdoor walking after lifting. No running, jumping, or high-impact intervals while the nerve is irritable.

5. Centralize, do not chase

Symptoms should stay put or move up the leg. If they spread farther down, that drill is done for the day.

6. Range is pain-free

Cut the squat, hinge, or walk short of the flare. Depth is not a virtue here. Clean reps are.

Every session, in this order

Warm-up · 8–10 minutes
  • Easy walk, 5 minutes. You can speak in full sentences.
  • 90/90 breathing, 5 slow breaths. Ribs stacked over the pelvis.
  • Pelvic tilts, 8. Find the middle. That middle is your training posture.
  • Glute squeezes, 10 holds of 3 seconds. Then a dowel hip hinge, 8 reps.
Finish · 15–30 minutes
  • Main lifts, 35–45 minutes. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets.
  • Easy walk as written for that day. No incline that recreates leg pain.
  • Cool-down: 5 minutes of slower walking, then a supine figure-four if the hip allows it.
  • If sitting flares your symptoms, stand or lie between sets. Do not sit in a slump.
Saturday and Sunday are rest. Optional easy walking is fine. Five quality days will beat seven sloppy ones.

Trainer note on 2,400 calories

The plan includes a 2,400-calorie, protein-emphasized template. At 5 feet 1 inch and 165 lb, estimated daily burn with this training is roughly 1,940–2,190 calories. Eating 2,400 calories is likely a small surplus. A surplus will not produce a 30-pound fat-loss phase.

Hold protein at 150–165 grams either way. That is about 0.9–1.0 gram per pound of current body weight, and it is the amount that protects muscle. If the scale has not moved after 14 days at 2,400, drop toward 1,750–1,850 without touching the protein.

Stop and seek urgent care New bowel or bladder changes, numbness in the saddle area (inner thighs, genitals, anus), or rapidly worsening leg weakness are not training problems. They are medical emergencies. Fever with back pain also needs a physician, not another set of bridges.

Leave these out for now

  • Sit-ups, crunches, V-ups, and Russian twists
  • Toe touches, standing forward folds, and aggressive hamstring stretches that recreate leg pain
  • Bent-over rows and heavy conventional deadlifts from the floor
  • Running, jumping, burpees, and box jumps
  • Kettlebell swings, snatches, cleans, and windmills
  • Double-leg lifts and hanging leg raises
  • Deep yoga flexion if it sends symptoms down the leg
  • Long sits between sets, in the car, and on the sofa
  • Pushing a walk, squat, or hinge into the flare “to work through it”
  • Loading a lift whose form you cannot control for every rep
  • Stretching a nerve that is already angry. Glide it. Do not yank it.

Recovery days

Saturday and Sunday are rest days. Optional 20–40 minutes of easy walking is fine if symptoms are stable, then the nerve glide if it does not flare your symptoms. Sleep 7–9 hours. Heat may ease muscle guarding; ice is optional. If a session spikes leg symptoms for more than 24 hours, the next session is a walk and the McGill trio only.
